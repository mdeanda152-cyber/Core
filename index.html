This is the actual trade the formula turns on. Ice cream scoops when enough of its water stays liquid at freezer temperature, and that is set by how many molecules of solute are dissolved — not how many grams. Allulose is the lever because it weighs 180 g/mol against sucrose's 342, so it depresses the freezing point about 1.9× as hard per gram, at 0.4 kcal/g.

At 18 g allulose and 10 g fat the base sits at the firm end of the premium window — scoopable from a home freezer, still holds a shape on a cone.

The calculated figure runs a point or two above our 335 target — dairy lot composition moves it by a few calories either way, and the printed panel will come from assay rather than from this model. Our rejected v0.9 ran 26 g allulose plus glycerol and landed near 40 — gummy and slumping. Cutting back freed about 16 calories, which we spent on butterfat. That trade is why texture outranks macros in our priority stack.

Diagnosis three broken systems

Why protein ice cream tastes like protein ice cream

Not bad luck or cheap ingredients. Pulling sugar and fat out of a frozen emulsion breaks three independent physical systems at once, and most brands patch only one.

Sugar → structure

Remove the sugar, freeze a brick

Sugar's main job is not sweetness, it is freezing-point depression. Dissolved sugar keeps part of the water liquid at freezer temperature, and that unfrozen phase is what lets a scoop yield.

Take it out and more water turns to ice, so the pint comes out at −18 °C as something you have to microwave. That is the original Halo Top complaint.2

Fat → network

Remove the fat, lose the scaffold

Fat globules partially coalesce under shear in the freezer barrel and knit a semi-solid network around every air bubble. That network is what makes a scoop stand up, cut cleanly and melt slowly.

Near-zero-fat products have no network. They eat wet, collapse into a puddle, and lose the fat-soluble aromas that carry vanilla, cocoa and nut.

Air → dilution

Whip in air, dilute the pint

Air is the cheapest way to cut calories per pint, because a pint is a fixed volume. Halo Top runs about 82% overrun against 20–30% for premium ice cream.2

The cost is real: a foamier pint carries less of everything per bite, delivers less flavour, and melts faster. You are buying air at ice-cream prices.

Decisions two we will not move on

What is deliberately in, and out

Deliberately absent

No erythritol, not one gram

It tastes cold — a strongly negative heat of solution pulls heat from your mouth as it dissolves, producing a menthol-like burn that fights every warm note you want in ice cream. It recrystallises gritty over the freeze–thaw cycles of real distribution. And a 2023 Nature Medicine study linked circulating erythritol to major adverse cardiovascular events across three cohorts, with a platelet-reactivity mechanism reinforced by 2024 intervention work.5

Said fairly: that evidence is observational plus mechanistic, not a randomised outcome trial, critics note platelet aggregation is an imperfect predictor of individual risk, and no regulator has restricted it. We are not calling it dangerous. But when an ingredient is contested and tastes cold and goes gritty, and allulose beats it on all three, the choice makes itself.

Deliberately present

Six grams of real sugar

Kept against considerable pressure to claim zero. Sucrose has a sweetness onset and decay curve that no substitute reproduces — it arrives immediately and leaves cleanly, where stevia and monk fruit both arrive late and linger. A small amount of real sugar anchors the front of the curve and makes the high-intensity sweeteners read as sweetness rather than as sweeteners.

"Zero sugar" is a macro win and a taste loss. Taste is first. Six grams stays.

Operating principle the order is the strategy

Every argument resolves against this stack, top down

When two of them conflict, the higher one wins and we write down what it cost us.

First

Taste

If a change makes the macros better and the pint worse, the change is rejected. No exceptions.

Second

Texture

Crystal size, melt rate, scoop resistance, heat-shock survival. Measured, not guessed.

Third

Macros

335 and 42 are targets, not commandments. A flavour that needs 370 calories to be excellent gets 370.

Fourth

Cost

Allulose and Bronte pistachio are expensive. We pay it, price accordingly, find margin elsewhere.
