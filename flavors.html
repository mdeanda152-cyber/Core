Launch eight  ·  335–385 kcal  ·  40–43 g protein

Eight flavours. One of them is a dare.

Chocolate, peanut butter and cookie dough forgive almost anything — bitterness, chalk, off-notes all disappear under enough cocoa. Vanilla forgives nothing. So vanilla is the flagship, and every other flavour is built on the base that had to survive it.

Flagship

Toasted Milk & Vanilla Bean

Dulce de leche on the front, Bourbon vanilla through the middle, a clean salted finish. Visible seed specks. Custard-style — this base carries egg yolk.

Vanilla has nowhere to hide protein. We solved it by roasting the milk powder to hazelnut brown so the dairy note reads as caramelised milk rather than supplement. If this one works, the platform works.

335 kcal42g P10g F6g sugar

Dark Chocolate Truffle

Deep and slightly bitter, closer to a set ganache than to milk chocolate. 72% couverture melted into the hot mix, with Dutched cocoa for colour and depth and natural cocoa for acidity and lift.

Cocoa solids bring their own protein and starch, so this base runs higher on both. Cocoa polyphenols are astringent, which conveniently masks residual whey astringency — the easiest flavour in the range.

345 kcal43g P11g F7g sugar

Salted Butter Caramel

Taken to the bitter edge before the cream goes in — properly dark, not sweet-toffee. Browned butter, Guérande salt, a caramel ripple through the pint.

Only possible because allulose is a real reducing sugar that genuinely caramelises. No polyol and no high-intensity sweetener will brown, which is why every other low-sugar caramel tastes of caramel flavouring.

360 kcal41g P11g F9g sugar

Coffee & Cream

Cold-brew concentrate from a medium-dark Brazilian, steeped rather than roasted into the mix. Bittersweet, low acid, long finish.

Cold brew is the right call at low fat — hot extraction pulls chlorogenic acid degradation products that read harsh when there is no fat to round them. Coffee's bitterness also covers the sweetener tail.

335 kcal42g P10g F6g sugar

Strawberries & Cultured Cream

Halved and roasted to concentrate, then folded in as a ripple, against a crème fraîche base. Tastes like strawberries and cream, not like strawberry milkshake.

The hardest of the eight. Fruit acid drives casein toward its isoelectric point and risks a grainy mix, so the fruit goes in cold and late as a ripple, never into the pasteurised base. The tang comes from the culture, which is pH-safe.

340 kcal41g P10g F8g sugar

Peanut Butter Fudge Ripple

High-roast peanut, salted hard, with a dark fudge ripple that stays soft at scooping temperature. The richest thing we make.

The ripple carries real fat and sugar and pushes this pint to 385 calories. We could make a leaner version. It would be a worse flavour, so we didn't.

385 kcal43g P15g F10g sugar

Cookies & Cultured Cream

Cultured base against a dark cocoa biscuit baked for us and crushed coarse, so the pieces stay crisp at the centre and soften only at the edges.

Inclusion water activity is the whole game. A biscuit that equilibrates with the ice cream around it goes soft and pasty within a week, so the crumb is baked dry and enrobed in cocoa butter to slow moisture migration.

375 kcal41g P13g F11g sugar
Chef series

Pistachio & Olive Oil

Bronte pistachio paste, a thread of grassy Coratina olive oil, flaked salt on top. Savoury, green, adult. Naturally khaki — we don't colour it.

The halo product. Nut paste brings its own fat and protein, and the olive oil polyphenols add a peppery bitterness that makes it read as a restaurant dessert rather than a diet product. Our most expensive pint by a distance.

On the numbers why they differ

The macros aren't identical across the line

Most of the category prints one number on every flavour. That is either a very loose rounding convention or a signal that the inclusions are doing very little.

A fudge ripple is fat and sugar. A pistachio paste is fat and protein. A cocoa biscuit is starch and butter. If you add them to a base and the panel doesn't move, you didn't add enough of them to taste. Our base is 335 calories and 42 g of protein; flavours run 335–385 calories and 40–43 g of protein, and we print each one.

The rule we hold to

No flavour ships above 400 calories per pint, and none ships below 40 g of protein. Inside that box, the flavour gets whatever it needs. Outside it, we either fix the flavour or we don't launch it.
