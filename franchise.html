Phase 0  ·  formulation lock  ·  no units trading

Franchise nationwide. Eventually. In that order.

The ambition is a Guiltless in every market. The sequence matters more than the ambition: a franchise system built on a product that isn't finished, or unit economics that haven't been proven with our own money, is a machine for scaling a mistake.

Sequencing shops before shelf

Why the scoop shop comes before the pint

Counter-intuitive for a CPG-shaped product. There are four reasons, and three of them are about the recipe rather than the money.

Product

It is at its best there

Every texture claim we make is easiest to hit behind a counter: the ice cream is days old, has never left a controlled cabinet, and is served at −13 °C instead of surviving a delivery van and a domestic freezer. Launching where the product is strongest means the first impression is the real one.

Research

Iteration is 50× faster

Reformulating a retail pint is a nine-month cycle — panel, co-man trial, artwork, distributor reset. A batch freezer behind a counter is an afternoon. Two variants a week against real paying customers is a research programme no CPG brand can buy.

Margin

It funds the ingredient deck

Allulose, Reb M and Bronte pistachio are expensive, and grocery shelf pricing punishes that. Scoop shop gross margin absorbs a premium ingredient deck that a $6.99 pint simply cannot — which means we never have to value-engineer the recipe to survive.

Brand

Memory beats the panel

A pint from a brand nobody has tasted competes on the nutrition panel alone — a race we would eventually lose to someone with a bigger co-man. A pint from a brand you queued for competes on memory. Shops build the thing that makes the retail launch work.

The two numbers that decide whether this works

Price realisation

$6.50–$7.50 a scoop

And $12–$14 for a shop pint. That is above a conventional creamery and roughly at the top of the artisanal band. The bet is that 42 g of protein converts an occasional indulgence into a several-times-a-week purchase — which changes visit frequency far more than it changes ticket.

Frequency

Week six, not week one

Every high-protein dessert brand gets a novelty spike. The whole business is what week six looks like. We track repeat rate at 30 and 90 days as the primary metric — ahead of revenue — because a shop that sells on novelty is not a shop you can franchise.

Roadmap gates, not dates

What has to be true before the next phase starts

We would rather be late to phase four than arrive there with a system that doesn't replicate.

  1. Phase 0 — Lock base v1.0

    Formulation frozen, third-party analytical verification of the nutrition panel, trained sensory panel beating the leading protein pints on a blind triangle test, and heat-shock survival demonstrated across six accelerated freeze–thaw cycles.

    gate: panel win + verified panel + < 25 µm after cycling

  2. Phase 1 — One shop, one market

    A single flagship, company-owned, run by the founding team. The goal is not profit — it is the operating manual: batch schedules, tempering discipline, waste rates, the training curve for a new scooper, and honest data on which flavours actually reorder.

    gate: 30- and 90-day repeat rate above target

  3. Phase 2 — Prove it replicates

    Three to five company-owned shops in the same metro, deliberately not all in premium locations. Replication risk is the entire question a franchisee is buying an answer to, and the only honest way to answer it is with our own capital first.

    gate: 3 consecutive units hitting AUV and EBITDA targets

  4. Phase 3 — Retail pints, regionally

    Co-manufactured pints into a single region where the shops have already built awareness. Retail is where the cold chain gets hard — if a retail pint cannot match the counter, it does not ship. We would rather stay a scoop shop longer than put a worse product in the same packaging.

    gate: retail pint matches shop pint on blind panel

  5. Phase 4 — Franchise

    FDD prepared and registered, Item 19 financial performance representation built on real company-owned results, franchisee support infrastructure hired ahead of the first sale. Then, and only then, the first territory.

    gate: registered FDD + support team in place

Defensibility the recipe is not the moat

A recipe isn't a moat. Four things around it are.

01

Process knowledge

The ingredient list is on this website. The ageing discipline, destabilisation curve and hardening protocol that turn it into the actual product are not on anyone's website, and they take a year of batches to learn.

02

The feedback loop

Every shop is a research site. A competitor with one co-man run per quarter cannot iterate against ours, and the gap compounds with each location.

03

Ingredient supply

Allulose and Reb M have narrow supply bases. Contracted volume at scale is a genuine cost and availability advantage, and it gets harder to replicate as we grow.

04

Being trusted on taste

This category has trained people to expect disappointment. Being the brand that reliably isn't disappointing is a position you earn once, slowly, and it is very hard to take.
